# `C_ASSOC` market-proxy specification

Date: 2026-07-30

Status: rule crosswalk complete; computational line not yet admitted to a
confirmatory frontier because the v4 runtime gate is closed.

## 1. Purpose and naming discipline

`C_ASSOC` is a CAISO-style storage ancillary-service proxy. It is not a claim
to reproduce CAISO production software, hidden validation logic, settlement,
or every Business Practice Manual rule. Its purpose is to anchor the
information-contract comparison to current primary-source requirements rather
than compare only author-designed abstractions.

## 3. Frozen proxy semantics

The first `C_ASSOC` implementation will represent the MFE generic reserve as
the 30-minute Spinning/Non-Spinning endurance case, not day-ahead Regulation.
Using the 60-minute Regulation rule would be a separate product variant and
must not be silently mixed into the same line.

The operator-facing tuple is

\[
  (h_t,r_t^+,r_t^-,\widehat e_t,
    \bar P^{dis},\bar P^{ch},\tau),
\]

where `h` and aggregate reserve remain the existing static award fields and
`\widehat e_t` is a declared or market-computed start-of-hour battery SOC
point. For the conservative aggregate proxy, the entire hybrid reserve award
is treated as battery-backed; renewable curtailment headroom is not credited.

The minimum registered checks are:

\[
0\le \widehat e_t\le E^{max},
\]

\[
\frac{r_t^+\tau}{\eta_d}
\le \widehat e_t-E^{min},
\qquad
\eta_c r_t^-\tau
\le E^{max}-\widehat e_t,
\]

together with the registered direction-specific instantaneous MW and
ramp-to-award limits. The SOC point must be generated by a documented
day-ahead battery schedule and the same efficiency convention; it cannot be
chosen independently hour by hour merely to pass each check.

These inequalities are a market-style sufficiency screen. They are not
asserted to be the exact projection of PV+BESS recourse, and they do not
credit:

- upward reserve from releasing scheduled PV curtailment;
- upward reserve from reducing battery charging before discharging;
- downward reserve from reducing battery discharge;
- sequential SOC consequences after an actual call;
- stochastic source correspondence.

This conservatism is intentional and must be measured, not hidden.

## 4. Hybrid-resource attribution caveat

CAISO's storage SOC rule is naturally applied to an identified storage
resource and its award. In an aggregate PV+BESS tuple, the same reserve MW can
come from battery energy, reduced charging, or renewable headroom. Therefore
one aggregate `r` field is not enough to apply a source-specific SOC rule
without an attribution convention.

The primary `C_ASSOC` line uses the conservative convention “all reserve is
battery-backed.” A secondary `C_ASSOC+source` sensitivity may expose
`r^{+,B}`, `r^{-,B}`, and renewable headroom, but it is a richer Chameleon
contract, not a representation of the unmodified market baseline. It may not
replace the primary line after viewing outcome gaps.

## 5. Admission tests before numerical use

1. one hand-checkable 24-hour deterministic case;
2. independent reconstruction of every `\widehat e_t`;
3. isolated 30-minute up/down activation replay;
4. rejection of an award that passes power headroom but fails SOC endurance;
5. clear separation from the 60-minute Regulation variant;
6. zero claims that the proxy reproduces the complete CAISO market engine.

Until these tests pass, `C_ASSOC` is a documented baseline specification only,
not a plotted experimental result.
