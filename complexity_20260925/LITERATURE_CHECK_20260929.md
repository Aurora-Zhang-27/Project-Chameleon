# Literature check: exact storage deliverability and related tools

Initial check 2026-09-29; additions October7,9,11,12. English publication edition, 2026-10-14. This is a **historical source-status record**, not a fresh October14 literature search. Links identify the sources checked then; availability and citation counts may change.

Scope: Proposition 1,Theorem 2,Lemma 3,Theorem 4,ExamplesA/B in [the main document](N3_THEOREM_N4_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md), plus homogeneous fleets and affine single-state tests. Earlier research-gate and N2 search notes remain in the complete local archive, outside this minimal publication.

**Conclusions:** sandwich separation, vector convolution and singleton/full-set tightening are known tools. No identical precedent was found in checked sources for Proposition 1,the three-dimensional pair formula and tight-modularity result,the N≤3/N=4 boundary,Theorem 4,Example B,Theorem H,Example D,the coefficient-2 facets or the particular affine failure certificate/systematic tests. “Not found” is not exhaustive exclusion or confirmed priority. Optimal balancing for proportional fleets and affine/lifted policy methods are already known.

## 1. Proposition 1 and discrete convexity

The input K=P(b)∩Q(a) requires b submodular,a supermodular, but no crossing condition. Adding P_y yields mixed inequalities

    e(A)−e(B)≤b(A∪M)−a(B∪M)+D(A)+C(B).

Murota's operations survey1907.09161,Table 4, discusses M♮ closure under Minkowski sums,projection,restriction and integer-box intersection, but not general intersection or the special sandwich-plus-M class here. The reviewed passages did not supply Proposition 1.

Murota–Tamura2312.01822,Theorem 3.1, cites Danilov–Koshevoy: for a unimodular matrix A,polyhedra whose face spans are generated by subsets of A's columns form a Minkowski-sum-closed class. This concerns **edge/span directions**, not directly facet normals. Our K can have edge(1,−1,−1),outside the M♮ direction system; all subset vectors are not unimodular at N≥3,as det[1₁₂,1₁₃,1₂₃]=−2. Therefore that theorem does not directly imply Proposition 1. It suggests why more general subset inputs can generate directions such as(2,1,1).

Moriguchi–Murota2110.10445,Theorem 4.1, describes a sum of two L-convex sets with x(J)−x(I) inequalities,where |I|=|J| and I∩J=∅. Its appearance is similar but its class and balanced-cardinality constraint differ. Our object is a special continuous M♮₂ subclass plus an M-type box slice,allowing arbitrary disjoint A,B. The checked papers did not give this description.

## 2. Tight-bound dimension boundary

Frank–Király–Pap–Pritchard,EGRES TR-2012-03/Mathematical Programming2014,characterizes g-polymatroids through totally dual laminar descriptions. It studies recognition of that class,not tight-bound submodularity for sandwich sets that may fail crossing. No dimension-three/four result was found in the checked report.

Moriguchi–Murota §6.3 defines continuous M♮₂ polyhedra as intersections of two g-polymatroids;descriptions take max/min and need not remain super/submodular. H3″ is a **proper subclass**,not synonymous with M♮₂. The checked passages and targeted searches for intersections with dimension-three/four terminology did not yield Lemma 3(c)(d).

Bisubmodular descriptions also use mixed signs,but the proposed edge-direction argument excluding that class relied on an edge characterization not verified from its original source in this search. Do not present that argument as a checked theorem application without reading the original Ando–Fujishige/Fujishige result.

## 4. Complexity and control analogies

Atamtürk–Zhang 2007 studies two-stage robust network flow,with hard separation in some networks and tractable ordered/tree cases. Gottschalk et al.2018 studies robust flows over time and hard feasibility verification. Both are background,read at abstract/scope level;neither is a proved reduction for this multistage shared-request storage problem. General robust invariant/viability methods use projection,lifting or inner/outer approximations;no applicable fixed-size storage structure theorem was found in the checked material.

## 5. Access limitations

Frank 2011,Fujishige 2005 and Murota 2003 books were not available in full. The downloaded Frank item was a two-page publication announcement,not the book. Relevant book topics include vector reduction,real separation,box/slab intersections and least-majorized bases. Frank 1982 original full text and its theorem number were not obtained;the real formula was first verified in Murota's slides and later in Frank–Tardos 1988. Some journal versions were unavailable,so preprints were used. Search mainly covered English open resources,not an exhaustive international literature collection.

These limitations affect priority certainty,not the self-contained derivations in the theorem documents. Do not imply that a search hit,a publisher abstract or an unavailable book was read in full.

## 6. Suggested paper framing

Use separation,convolution and singleton/full-set lemmas as preliminaries. Possible structural contributions are the exact N≤3 recursion,the N4 failure mechanism,the explicit single-request description and the three-dimensional tightening result. Homogeneous initial-state recursion should be distinguished from Evans' known balancing policy. Include exact projection methods and existing affine numerical observations in comparisons. Use “no precedent found in the checked literature”;complete remaining priority checks before a stronger claim.

Lemma S resembles II.2.10's upper min(b(X),β−p(S∖X)),but replaces crossing with symmetry/concavity. F3 follows II.2.11 with absent lower box bound. Theorems remain self-contained,while source attribution distinguishes the known tools.

**Guo–Wei–Chen–Shahidehpour–Mei**,IEEE TPS37(1):738–749,2022,*Distribution System Operation With Renewables and Energy Storage: A Linear Programming Based Multistage Robust Feasibility Approach*,was read in full. It studies distribution networks with multiple storage devices and per-period box renewable uncertainty. Dynamic robust feasible regions are recursively defined backward:Claim 1 checks uncertainty vertices;Claim 2 establishes polyhedrality;Claim 3 gives an H-description via dual vertices. Since full enumeration is impractical,the algorithm adds cuts using a bilinear separation subproblem. This is an exact projection framework under exact subproblem solution,but no polynomial bound on faces/iterations is supplied. It does not give this dimension boundary or a fixed-size closed representation. Do not infer that four devices lack **every** compact exact representation;our counterexample refutes the subset/template family,not all algorithms.

Zhai-group work therefore did not overlap the particular fleet exactness results in the checked formulas. The affine literature position also remains scoped as above. Unavailable Frank/Fujishige books were replaced as tool sources by the checked1988/1998 papers;their remaining priority content is still unverified.
